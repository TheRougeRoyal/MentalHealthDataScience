1. Not a Medical Device

The Mental Health Risk Assessment System (MHRAS) is a clinical decision-support tool. MHRAS is not a medical device as defined by the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (21 U.S.C. §321). MHRAS has not been reviewed, cleared, or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the diagnosis, treatment, cure, or prevention of any disease or medical condition.

2. Not a Substitute for Professional Medical Judgment

MHRAS DOES NOT DIAGNOSE MENTAL HEALTH CONDITIONS. MHRAS DOES NOT PROVIDE TREATMENT RECOMMENDATIONS. ALL OUTPUTS FROM MHRAS ARE CLINICAL DECISION-SUPPORT INFORMATION ONLY AND MUST BE EVALUATED BY A QUALIFIED HEALTHCARE PROFESSIONAL.

MHRAS uses a clinical rules engine and risk-modeling algorithms to process patient screening data and generate risk assessments, contributing factors, and clinical interpretations. These outputs are intended solely as decision-support tools for licensed healthcare professionals and must not be used as a substitute for:

  • Professional clinical judgment
  • A comprehensive clinical evaluation
  • A licensed clinician's assessment of a patient's mental health status
  • Established diagnostic criteria (DSM-5-TR, ICD-11)
  • Direct patient-clinician interaction
  • Evidence-based treatment planning

3. No Diagnostic Capability

MHRAS does not and cannot:

  • Diagnose any mental health disorder, condition, or illness
  • Determine the presence or absence of suicidal ideation with clinical certainty
  • Prescribe, recommend, or contraindicate any medication or treatment
  • Replace a comprehensive psychiatric evaluation
  • Assess cognitive function, personality, or behavioral patterns
  • Evaluate the adequacy of a patient's current treatment plan
  • Provide prognostic information about disease progression or outcomes
  • Determine a patient's capacity to consent to treatment

4. Limitations of the Clinical Rules Engine

The MHRAS clinical rules engine has inherent limitations that users must understand:

4.1 Algorithm Limitations

  • Risk scores are probabilistic estimates, not deterministic measurements
  • The rules engine operates on structured data inputs and cannot account for all clinical variables
  • Assessment accuracy depends on the quality and completeness of input data
  • The system cannot detect nuances conveyed through non-verbal clinical observation
  • Cultural, linguistic, and socioeconomic factors may affect assessment accuracy
  • The system may not adequately capture atypical presentations of mental health conditions

4.2 Data Limitations

  • PHQ-9 and GAD-7 are screening instruments, not diagnostic tools
  • Wearable biometric data provides indirect indicators, not direct clinical measurements
  • EMR data may be incomplete, outdated, or inconsistent across sources
  • Patient self-report data may be inaccurate due to stigma, recall bias, or intentional misrepresentation
  • The system cannot verify the accuracy or completeness of input data

4.3 Population Limitations

  • The clinical rules engine was developed and validated on specific patient populations
  • Performance may vary across demographic groups, age ranges, and clinical populations
  • The system has not been validated for all mental health conditions
  • Cross-cultural applicability has not been fully established

Healthcare professionals using MHRAS bear sole responsibility for:

  • Interpreting system outputs within the context of the complete clinical picture
  • Exercising independent clinical judgment regarding all patient care decisions
  • Verifying the accuracy and appropriateness of system-generated assessments
  • Considering patient history, comorbidities, and individual circumstances not captured by the system
  • Ensuring that system outputs are consistent with established clinical guidelines
  • Making final determinations regarding diagnosis, treatment, and disposition
  • Following up on all flagged high-risk and critical-risk assessments in a clinically appropriate timeframe
  • Adhering to all applicable standards of care and professional obligations

6. Emergency Situations

MHRAS IS NOT AN EMERGENCY RESPONSE SYSTEM. IN ANY SITUATION INVOLVING IMMINENT RISK OF HARM TO SELF OR OTHERS, IMMEDIATELY CONTACT EMERGENCY SERVICES (911) AND THE 988 SUICIDE & CRISIS LIFELINE. DO NOT RELY ON MHRAS OUTPUTS FOR EMERGENCY CLINICAL DECISIONS.

MHRAS includes automated flagging of high-risk and critical-risk assessments. However:

  • System flags do not constitute emergency notifications
  • The system does not directly contact emergency services or crisis resources
  • There may be a delay between screening and clinical review of flagged cases
  • The system may not identify all patients at imminent risk
  • Clinical judgment must always supersede system outputs in emergency situations
