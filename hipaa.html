HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices

Last Updated: July 19, 2026

THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION. PLEASE REVIEW IT CAREFULLY.

1. About This Notice

This Notice of Privacy Practices ("Notice") describes how the Mental Health Risk Assessment System ("MHRAS," "the System," "we," "us," or "our") may use and disclose your protected health information (PHI) and explains your rights regarding your health information under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 ("HIPAA"), the HITECH Act, and applicable state privacy laws.

We are required by law to:

  • Maintain the privacy of your protected health information
  • Provide you with this Notice of our legal duties and privacy practices
  • Follow the terms of the Notice that is currently in effect
  • Notify you in the event of a breach of your unsecured protected health information

We reserve the right to change our privacy practices and the terms of this Notice at any time, provided that the changes are permitted by applicable law. Any changes will apply to all PHI we maintain. A copy of our current Notice is available at https://mhras.example.com/hipaa.html.

2. How We May Use and Disclose Your Health Information

The following describes the ways we may use and disclose your PHI:

2.1 Treatment

We may use and disclose your PHI to provide, coordinate, and manage your healthcare. For example:

  • Processing screening data to generate risk assessments for your treatment team
  • Sharing assessment results with authorized clinicians involved in your care
  • Facilitating clinical review workflows for flagged high-risk cases
  • Coordinating care with other healthcare providers through System outputs

2.2 Healthcare Operations

We may use and disclose your PHI for healthcare operations, including:

  • Quality assessment and improvement activities
  • Patient safety activities
  • Provider credentialing and training
  • System auditing and compliance programs
  • Case management and care coordination

2.3 Appointment Reminders

We may use your PHI to contact you for appointment reminders or to inform you of treatment alternatives or health-related benefits that may be of interest to you.

2.4 Required by Law

We will use and disclose your PHI when required to do so by federal, state, or local law, including:

  • Reporting to public health authorities for disease control
  • Reporting suspected abuse, neglect, or domestic violence
  • Responding to court orders, subpoenas, or other lawful process
  • Reporting to law enforcement under certain limited circumstances
  • Complying with workers' compensation laws

2.5 Public Health and Safety

We may disclose your PHI for public health and safety purposes, including:

  • Preventing or lessening a serious threat to health or safety
  • Reporting adverse reactions to medications or medical devices
  • Notifying individuals exposed to a communicable disease
  • Reporting to the FDA regarding product problems

2.6 Health Oversight Activities

We may disclose your PHI to health oversight agencies for authorized activities including audits, investigations, inspections, and licensure.

2.7 Law Enforcement

We may disclose your PHI to law enforcement officials as required or permitted by law, including:

  • In compliance with a court order, court-ordered warrant, or grand jury subpoena
  • In response to an administrative request or subpoena
  • To report certain types of wounds, injuries, or crimes
  • In emergency circumstances to identify or locate a suspect, fugitive, or missing person

2.8 Organ and Tissue Donation

If you are an organ donor, we may disclose your PHI to organ procurement organizations as authorized by law.

2.9 Research

We may use or disclose your PHI for research purposes when the research has been approved by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or Privacy Board that has reviewed the research protocol and determined that adequate privacy safeguards are in place. Research may also be conducted on de-identified data that does not constitute PHI.

2.10 Workers' Compensation

We may disclose your PHI as authorized by workers' compensation or similar laws.

3. Uses and Disclosures Requiring Your Written Authorization

We will obtain your written authorization before using or disclosing your PHI for purposes other than those described above, including:

  • Marketing: Uses and disclosures for marketing purposes (excluding face-to-face communications and promotional gifts of nominal value)
  • Sale of PHI: Any sale of your protected health information
  • Psychotherapy Notes: Most uses and disclosures of psychotherapy notes
  • Research: Research not otherwise permitted by this Notice or HIPAA

You may revoke your authorization at any time by submitting a written request to our Privacy Officer. Revocation will not affect any actions we took in reliance on the authorization before we received your revocation.

4. Your Rights Regarding Your Health Information

4.1 Right to Access

You have the right to inspect and obtain copies of your PHI maintained in the System. Requests must be submitted in writing. We will respond within 30 days. We may charge a reasonable fee for copies as permitted by applicable law.

4.2 Right to Request Amendment

You have the right to request amendment of your PHI if you believe it is incorrect or incomplete. Requests must be submitted in writing with a reason for the amendment. We may deny the request under certain circumstances and will provide a written explanation if we do so.

4.3 Right to an Accounting of Disclosures

You have the right to request an accounting of certain disclosures of your PHI made in the six years prior to your request. This does not include disclosures made for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations, or disclosures you authorized in writing.

4.4 Right to Request Restrictions

You have the right to request restrictions on certain uses and disclosures of your PHI. We are not required to agree to your request unless the disclosure is to a health plan for purposes of payment or healthcare operations and the PHI relates solely to services for which you have paid out of pocket in full.

4.5 Right to Request Confidential Communications

You have the right to request that we communicate with you about your health information through a specific method or at a specific location. For example, you may request that we contact you only by mail or at a specific phone number.

4.6 Right to a Paper Copy of This Notice

You have the right to obtain a paper copy of this Notice upon request, even if you have agreed to receive it electronically.

4.7 Right to Be Notified of a Breach

You have the right to be notified if there is a breach of your unsecured PHI, as required by the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule and applicable state breach notification laws.

5. Our Duties

We are required by law to:

  • Maintain the privacy of your PHI as required by law
  • Provide you with this Notice of our legal duties and privacy practices
  • Abide by the terms of the Notice currently in effect
  • Notify you if we are unable to agree to a requested restriction on how your PHI is used or disclosed
  • Accommodate reasonable requests you make to communicate health information by alternative means or at alternative locations
  • Notify you in the event of a breach of your unsecured PHI

6. Mental Health Records Protections

ADDITIONAL PROTECTIONS APPLY TO MENTAL HEALTH RECORDS. Under many state laws, mental health records receive heightened confidentiality protections beyond those provided by HIPAA. Substance use disorder records may be subject to additional protections under 42 CFR Part 2.

Specifically, MHRAS implements the following additional safeguards for mental health data:

  • Separate consent requirements for disclosure of psychotherapy notes
  • Enhanced access controls limiting mental health data to authorized treatment personnel
  • Additional audit logging for all accesses to mental health screening data
  • Prohibition on use of mental health data for employment or insurance decisions
  • Special handling for crisis-level risk assessments and emergency notifications
